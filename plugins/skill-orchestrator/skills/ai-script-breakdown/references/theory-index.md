# Theory and Practice Index

Use these entries as compact decision lenses. Apply only when relevant, paraphrase in original wording, and never copy substantial passages. Record used IDs in `theory_trace`; expand the theory explanation only when the user asks.

## Dramatic structure and causality

- `TH-ARISTOTLE-ACTION` — Aristotle, *Poetics*. Read drama primarily as organized action: events should follow through probability or necessity; reversal and recognition matter when they change consequence and understanding.
- `TH-FREYTAG-FIVE-ACT` — Gustav Freytag, *Die Technik des Dramas*. Use the five-part rise/turn/fall lens when the material actually presents that pressure shape; do not treat the pyramid as universal.
- `TH-FIELD-THREE-ACT` — Syd Field, *Screenplay*. Use setup, confrontation, resolution, and major pivots as a diagnostic map when supported by the draft.
- `TH-GULINO-SEQUENCE` — Paul Joseph Gulino, *Screenwriting: The Sequence Approach*. Examine semi-independent sequences with local objectives and climaxes that accumulate into the whole.
- `TH-MCKEE-VALUE-CHANGE` — Robert McKee, *Story*. Inspect beats, scenes, sequences, acts, turning points, and value change; a scene should change a meaningful condition rather than merely deliver activity.

## Character and performance

- `TH-STANISLAVSKI-ACTION` — Konstantin Stanislavski, *An Actor Prepares* and *Building a Character*. Translate inner intention into objective, obstacle, action, adjustment, and physical behavior.
- `TH-WESTON-PLAYABLE` — Judith Weston, *Directing Actors*. Prefer playable verbs, facts, images, and circumstances over result-oriented emotional labels.

## Visual form, editing, and sound

- `TH-BORDWELL-FORM` — David Bordwell and Kristin Thompson, *Film Art*. Analyze mise-en-scène, cinematography, editing, and sound as interdependent systems that organize viewer attention and meaning.
- `TH-BLOCK-VISUAL` — Bruce Block, *The Visual Story*. Track space, line, shape, tone, color, movement, and rhythm through contrast and affinity.
- `TH-MURCH-CUT` — Walter Murch, *In the Blink of an Eye*. Judge cuts by emotion, story, rhythm, eyeline, spatial plane, and continuity rather than continuity alone.
- `TH-CHION-SOUND` — Michel Chion, *Audio-Vision*. Examine how sound adds, redirects, withholds, synchronizes, or separates meaning from the image.

## Animation-style preproduction

- `TH-DISNEY-ANIMATION` — Frank Thomas and Ollie Johnston, *The Illusion of Life*. Adapt staging, timing, anticipation, follow-through, exaggeration, and appeal to realistic AI performance without turning photoreal movement into cartoon motion.

## Use rules

- Evidence comes from the screenplay; theory organizes questions and tests.
- Prefer the smallest set of theory IDs that materially explains a decision.
- Never label a work as structurally defective solely because it does not match a named model.
- Separate bibliographic source, screenplay evidence, interpretation, and recommendation.
- Treat current AI-provider capabilities as unstable external facts. This skill remains provider-neutral and does not encode capability claims here.
