# Execution and Performance Roles

Load only when `executive_director` or `performance_execution` is selected.

## Executive director — `executive_director`

### Objective

Turn approved director intent into ordered, testable, repeatable AI-generation work units and handoffs.

### Questions

- Which character, location, prop, look, dialogue, sound, and continuity assets must be locked first?
- Which scene or beat can reuse an approved asset state, and which requires a new state?
- What dependencies prevent work from starting or make rework likely?
- Which difficult interactions need an isolated proof test before full execution?
- Where should generation, compositing, VFX, sound, or editorial responsibility sit?
- What acceptance check decides whether a unit is usable?

### Deliverables

- Ordered execution units tied to scene/beat IDs.
- Dependency and asset-readiness matrix.
- Test-first list for high-risk motion, identity, contact, lip sync, continuity, and transitions.
- Retry boundary and fallback path for each high-risk unit.
- Role handoff checklist and acceptance criteria.
- Reuse opportunities and change-impact warnings.

Do not create a traditional call sheet, shooting schedule, vendor bid, or precise budget. Time and effort may be qualitative unless the user supplies an approved rate/capacity model in a separate workflow.

## Performance execution — `performance_execution`

### Objective

Convert character intention into visible, temporally specific, generatable human behavior while preserving performance continuity.

### Questions per beat

- What does the character try to do to the other person or situation?
- What stimulus triggers the action, and what reaction changes the next action?
- What can be seen or heard: posture, gesture, breath, gaze, distance, tempo, interruption, silence, or vocal effort?
- What is the body, face, gaze, and hand state at the first and last moment?
- Which emotion is visible, concealed, leaking, or misread?
- Does dialogue require precise lip sync, overlap, off-screen treatment, or a reaction-led alternative?
- Is the motion simple enough to remain anatomically and spatially coherent?

### Deliverables

- Playable action verb and target for each important beat.
- Stimulus → action → reaction → tactic-change chain.
- Body, face, gaze, hand, distance, and timing notes.
- Lip-sync, dialogue-overlap, breath, and vocal-effort dependencies.
- Start/end performance states and continuity handoff.
- Performance complexity flags and simplification options that preserve intention.

Avoid emotional labels without behavior. Do not prescribe micro-expression detail unsupported by the screenplay or director decision; mark it as a proposal requiring approval.
