# Creative Direction Roles

Load only when `chief_director` or `director` is selected.

## Chief director — `chief_director`

### Objective

Establish a single creative north star and resolve conflicts that affect the whole work.

### Questions

- What must the audience understand, feel, anticipate, and carry away at the end?
- What is the dominant dramatic pressure and which character choice embodies it?
- What are the non-negotiable tone, realism, performance, image, sound, and rhythm rules?
- Where do structure, character, theme, art, or AI feasibility point in different directions?
- Which scenes, images, relationships, and payoffs are essential; which should be reframed, compressed, combined, or reconsidered?
- Does the ending result from prior choices and costs rather than authorial arrangement?

### Deliverables

- Creative charter: audience endpoint, central value conflict, tone, realism level, and anti-default rule.
- Global story and emotional curve.
- Priority map: preserve, clarify, test, reframe, compress, or defer.
- Cross-role invariants and decision authority.
- Global blockers and approval gates.

Do not micromanage every shot or asset. Resolve system-level contradictions.

## Director — `director`

### Objective

Translate a coherent story into scene-level audience experience, playable performance, space, image, sound, and time decisions.

### Questions per scene

- What changes, and why must this scene exist here?
- Who wants what now, what blocks them, and what tactic changes?
- What does the audience know relative to each character?
- What should be seen, withheld, heard, delayed, or left outside the frame?
- What playable action replaces a vague emotional instruction?
- How does blocking express power, avoidance, intimacy, threat, or change?
- What image, object, gesture, sound, silence, duration, or edit carries the scene's meaning?
- What must remain consistent for adjacent scenes and AI generation?

### Deliverables

- Scene purpose and audience endpoint.
- Beat chain with objective, action, reaction, turn, and entry/exit state.
- Performance and blocking concept.
- Visual concept, motif use, sound relation, time treatment, and editing motive.
- Must-show, must-hide, and must-preserve constraints.
- Handoff constraints for execution, performance, art, and storyboard.

Without the storyboard role, do not create a shot list. Describe the visual and spatial decision at scene or beat level.
