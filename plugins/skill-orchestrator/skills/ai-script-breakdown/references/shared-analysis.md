# Shared Dramatic Analysis

Run this layer for every approved scope before role-specific analysis.

## Reading order

1. Establish source coverage and extraction confidence.
2. Identify what form the material is actually using; do not choose a named structure in advance.
3. Trace observable events, decisions, state changes, and consequences.
4. Build the character, relationship, information, world-rule, motif, and asset ledgers.
5. Segment scenes into beats only when something materially changes.
6. Diagnose the earliest upstream break before listing downstream symptoms.

## Global dimensions

- **Story contract:** format, genre promises, tone, intended audience experience, duration pressure, and ending contract.
- **Narrative form:** acts, sequences, parallel lines, nonlinear order, episodic A/B/C stories, vertical-drama hook cycles, or another form supported by evidence.
- **Causal spine:** triggering conditions, character choices, consequences, forced next actions, obvious alternatives, coincidences, and unresolved causal gaps.
- **Character system:** want, need, fear, misbelief, strategy, cost, arc, entrance/exit state, and relationship power.
- **Information design:** point of view, audience knowledge, character knowledge, concealment, reveal, suspense, surprise, and dramatic irony.
- **Theme and values:** value conflicts made concrete through choices, costs, images, objects, gestures, or silence.
- **Setup and payoff:** planted facts, props, motifs, promises, reloads, reversals, and actual payoffs that change meaning or choice.
- **World rules:** physical, social, institutional, technological, supernatural, and stylistic rules; note violations and missing rules.
- **Rhythm:** pressure, pace, information density, emotional intensity, release, aftertaste, and full-work curve.
- **Continuity:** time, geography, physical state, possessions, wardrobe, knowledge, relationships, injuries, weather, and world state.

## Scene and beat dimensions

For each scene identify:

- Scene function and necessity.
- Entry and exit state.
- Active objective, obstacle, stakes, tactic, and tactic change.
- External and internal conflict.
- Information gained, lost, concealed, or reclassified.
- Relationship or power change.
- Value movement and turning point.
- Subtext and the action each consequential line attempts.
- Setup/payoff and continuity links.
- Primary emotion, intensity start/end, and rhythm.
- Downstream asset or role requirements.

Create a new beat only when objective, tactic, action, information, power, emotion, spatial state, gaze, or blocking changes materially. Do not split mechanically at each line.

## Adaptive theory use

- Use three-act, five-act, or sequence approaches only when they illuminate the observed structure.
- Use scene-value and beat analysis at the scale where actual change occurs.
- For nonlinear, ensemble, mosaic, or contemplative work, prefer causality, information, value, rhythm, and motif maps over forced act labels.
- For episodes, distinguish episode closure from season/series continuation.
- For vertical drama, examine hook, escalation, information reward, and cliffhanger density without treating density alone as quality.
- Record applied IDs from `theory-index.md` in `theory_trace`; do not turn the readable report into a lecture unless requested.

## Diagnosis contract

Every finding uses:

- `severity`: `blocker`, `high`, `medium`, or `low`.
- `observation`: what the evidence shows, without value-loaded filler.
- `impact`: what breaks or weakens for story, audience, role, continuity, or generation.
- `recommendation`: a concrete revision direction, decision, test, or production constraint.
- `certainty` and `confidence`: kept separate from severity.

Diagnose the earliest causal or state failure. Do not propose polished dialogue, visual decoration, or added exposition as a patch for a broken objective, trigger, choice, or knowledge state.

## Non-rewrite boundary

Recommendations may describe what must change and why. They must not silently replace dialogue or action text. If the user later requests rewriting, treat it as a separate authorized task and preserve the breakdown evidence.
